How to read it. Each ridge is one moment of audio. It leaves the source at the bottom and travels away, so the newest sound is nearest and largest; the horizon is the oldest thing still in memory.

A ridge's height at any point is how loud that track is, and its lateral position is where it sits in the stereo field. The vertical gradient inside each ridge is that track's spectrum — low content at the base, high content at the crest — so the spectrum always fills whatever height the moment has. Loudness scales it; it never truncates it.

The source meter at the bottom is the same cross-section at t = 0: frequency stacked vertically, spread left and right by pan. It is the mouth the waves come out of.

The cone. Waves leave a narrow mouth and widen as they age, so the field is an inverted cone. Whether it reads as expanding or as receding is set by Camera against Mouth: apparent width goes as (mouth + age) / (camera + age), so the cone only widens while the camera sits further back than the mouth is wide. Pull Camera in and perspective takes over; push it out and the cone runs away.

Nothing is clipped at the frame edge — Haze is what ends the cone, dissolving each wave as it recedes.

What to judge. Push Height up until near peaks start swallowing the history behind them — that boundary is the real design constraint. Then sweep Arc from 0 to 100 to see what the radiating contours cost and buy.
